What the valuation covers

Fair Value

Estimated realisable value of the machinery in an orderly sale between willing parties, as on the insolvency commencement date.

Liquidation Value

Estimated realisable value if the machinery is sold as part of liquidation, as on the same date.

Report

A clear, asset-wise report stating the premise of value, inspection findings, approach used and assumptions.

How the assignment works

  1. Engagement: The resolution professional or liquidator appoints the valuer and confirms scope and valuation date.
  2. Documents: You share the asset list and supporting records.
  3. Site inspection: Machinery is identified, inspected and checked for condition and working status.
  4. Analysis: Value is assessed using the cost approach, cross-checked with market evidence where available.
  5. Report: Fair value and liquidation value are presented in a structured report.

Documents usually needed

Fixed asset register, purchase invoices, list of machines with make and capacity, plant layout, maintenance and insurance records, and recent financial statements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who appoints the valuer in an IBC case?

Registered valuers are appointed by the resolution professional during the resolution process, or by the liquidator during liquidation, as per the IBC framework.

What is the difference between fair value and liquidation value?

Fair value is the estimated realisable value in an orderly transaction between willing parties. Liquidation value is the estimated realisable value if the assets are sold in liquidation.

Which documents are needed?

Typically the fixed asset register, purchase invoices, machine lists, plant layout, maintenance records and recent financial statements.
