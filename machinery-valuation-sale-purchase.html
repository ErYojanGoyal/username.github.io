How machinery value is assessed

A valuer looks at the make, model, capacity, age, condition, maintenance record, remaining useful life, technological obsolescence and current market demand. The value is usually worked out with the cost approach (replacement cost less depreciation and obsolescence) and cross-checked with the market approach, using prices of similar machines where available.

The report states the basis of value, the inspection findings, the method used and the assumptions, so both parties can see how the figure was reached.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a registered valuer to sell or buy machinery?

It is not always legally required, but a valuation by an independent IBBI registered valuer carries more credibility with buyers, banks and other parties than an informal estimate.

What is the difference between machinery value for sale and for liquidation?

Sale value assumes a normal, orderly sale with reasonable time. Liquidation value assumes a faster or forced sale and is usually lower.

Can you value used or second-hand machinery?

Yes. Condition, age, maintenance history and market demand are the main factors for used machinery.
